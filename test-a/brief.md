# One Small Puzzle — Test A production brief (prepared by Claude Code for Codex)

Derived from `references/one-small-puzzle-production-prompt.txt`. The "Puzzle type" section is replaced by the Type 2 paragraph from `references/one-small-puzzle-prompt-types.txt`, adapted from ten puzzles to one. Everything else keeps the production standard.

Act as the puzzle designer, visual designer and editor for One Small Puzzle, an English-language account for adults. Independently conceive, construct and art-direct **one** original puzzle of the type specified below. Claude Code operates the image API and the renderer for you (see the technical contract), but every editorial and visual decision about this puzzle is yours.

## Puzzle type

Illustrated observation and error spotting. Create one original illustrated scene in which the reader must find and explain one specific visual error, inconsistency or out-of-place detail. Establish what belongs through evidence visible in the scene, such as an object's function, a sequence of events or the relationship between two details. Target difficulty: **Medium or Hard** (your call, by adult standards). For a harder puzzle, make several details look plausible at first glance and let a second clue distinguish the answer. Keep the decisive clues visible at phone size and preserve the challenge through careful composition. Inspect the finished illustration for accidental errors that could create a second valid answer. In the private solution, identify the exact location, explain the contradiction and show why the strongest alternative fits the scene.

## Editorial brief

- Make the puzzle worthwhile for adults. Let difficulty come from what the reader must notice, combine or deduce. Tiny details, poor contrast, specialist trivia and lengthy repetitive work make a weak substitute for a good idea.
- Privately describe the solving step before you commit. If it differs from an existing catalogue entry (below) only in nouns, colours or numbers, rethink it.
- The puzzle must be self-contained and have a defensible answer. When the question promises one answer, make it unique under the public rules. Invent the actual instance and artwork.

### Existing One Small Puzzle catalogue to avoid (mechanism and subject)

Type 2 package (illustrated observation): beach parasols with one shadow in the wrong direction; three pairs of gloves, one pair with two same-hand gloves; three candles painted at 8 and 9 o'clock, one grew; theatre seats with a column blocking one sightline; greenhouse pipe valves (which bed stays dry); three pasted posters with a wrong overlap order; night-mail bag tags with a wrong arrival day after midnight; woodblock vs. printed impression with one unreversed stripe; crate stacks vs. front/side skyline scans; a hanging mobile with one unbalanced bar.
Type 1 package (impossible states): three glasses, a zip, a record needle, a book's missing leaf, a photo proof sheet, cassette tape trails, a shadow direction, an hourglass, sliding tiles, a punched ticket.
Reference account: a street scene with a school bus. Do not reproduce third-party puzzles.

## Art direction

Design this post individually. Choose the visual approach that makes this particular puzzle inviting and easy to understand: composition, viewpoint, medium and the relationship between text and image are yours to choose. Avoid a generic header/footer/card template. Give the puzzle as much of the frame as it needs. Keep all solving evidence readable at phone size (360 px wide).

## Deliverables

1. **One finished 1080 × 1080 PNG.** Include the question and any necessary rules where they help the reader solve it. The public image must be free of answers, solution annotations and difficulty labels. Illustration or image generation can supply a setting, while exact counts, markings, positions and other critical details can be drawn deterministically.
2. **One short, idiomatic English X caption.** Invite a specific response without giving away the insight. Avoid inflated success-rate claims, IQ bait and unnecessary hashtags.
3. **A private answer and complete explanation.** Show how a reader can reach the answer from the published information. Explain how you checked any reasonable competing answer.
4. **Brief editorial notes.** State the difficulty and why, the decisive solving step, and what must be checked in the finished image.

## Final checks

Solve the candidate before accepting it. Distinguish checks on the intended construction from observations on the rendered pixels. If the render introduces an unintended clue or another valid answer, it must be repaired. Describe difficulty as your own assessment; no human testing has occurred.
